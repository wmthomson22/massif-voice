# Gold Fields mounts charm offensive to woo Northern Star investors

Gold Fields has embarked on a charm offensive in Australia to try to convince shareholders in rival gold miner Northern Star to back its A$39bn ($27bn) takeover bid.

Mike Fraser, chief executive of the South African miner, has travelled to Perth, Sydney and Melbourne this week to meet investors and discuss the merits of its offer for Northern Star, which would create the world's largest gold producer by output if completed.

The all-share offer values Northern Star at a 28 per cent premium to its undisturbed share price. Northern Star's board has rejected the approach as opportunistic and undervaluing the company, pointing to the record gold price and the company's growth pipeline at its Kalgoorlie operations.

Fraser told investors the combination would deliver A$500mn of annual synergies and create a producer with 6mn ounces of annual output, diversified across Australia, South Africa, Ghana, Peru and Chile. "Scale matters in this industry," he said. "The generalist investor wants liquidity and a lower cost of capital, and that is what this combination delivers."

Analysts have noted that gold-sector M&A has accelerated as the metal has risen above $4,000 an ounce, with Newmont's acquisition of Newcrest and the AngloGold-Centamin deal setting precedents for consolidation. Some investors have questioned whether buyers are paying for reserves at the top of the price cycle.
